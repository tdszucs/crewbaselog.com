Keep your flying in one place and print it in the official EASA Part-FCL layout whenever a licensing authority, an airline or an examiner asks for your logbook.

What the EASA logbook PDF contains

CrewBase Log prints the columns of the EASA pilot logbook in FCL.050 and AMC1 FCL.050, numbered 1 to 12 as on the paper logbook:

  • Date, departure and arrival place and time, aircraft type and registration
  • Single-pilot time (SE / ME), multi-pilot time and total time of flight
  • Name of the PIC, day and night landings
  • Night and IFR time, PIC, co-pilot, dual and instructor time
  • FSTD (simulator) sessions with their type and time
  • Remarks and endorsements, including instructor signatures

Every page has total this page, total from previous pages and total time, and is signed with your saved signature under “I certify that the entries in this log are true”. Hours from an earlier paper logbook are carried in as previous experience, so the first page continues from your old totals.

You choose the full logbook, the last 10 pages (often asked for at airline interviews) or any range of dates, on A4 or US Letter.

Where the flights come from

  • Your airline's crew system: the eCrew / AIMS Pilot LogBook report
  • Your previous logbook app: Flylog, CrewLounge / mccPILOTLOG, LogTen, Safelog or a PDF logbook
  • Your own spreadsheet (CSV or Excel), or flights added by hand

Night time is worked out from the route and block times, duplicates are skipped, and a troubleshooting check points out lines that don't add up before you print.

Hours for job applications

The same logbook gives the figures airlines ask for: total, PIC, co-pilot, multi-crew, multi-engine, IFR and night time, by aircraft type and for the last 28 days to 24 months, ready to copy into an application form.
